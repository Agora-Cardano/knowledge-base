**ADA holders** are individuals or entities that own ADA, the native cryptocurrency of the Cardano blockchain. Their holdings provide the basis for participation in Cardano's stake-based governance system.

Under Cardano's governance framework, ADA holders can participate in decision-making by delegating their voting power to Delegated Representatives (DReps), registering as DReps themselves, or selecting predefined governance delegation options.

This article explains the governance rights of ADA holders, how voting power is distributed and delegated, and the methods and resources available for participating in governance.

## 1. ADA Holders in Cardano Governance

### Ownership and Governance Rights

ADA holders play a foundational role in Cardano governance because voting power is derived from the amount of ADA associated with eligible stake credentials.

The [Cardano Constitution](https://cardano.org/constitution/), under Article II, Section 2, establishes participation rights for ADA owners.

These include:

- **Governance participation:** The right to access and participate in on-chain decision-making processes.
- **Representation:** The ability to delegate voting power to registered DReps or register as a DRep.
- **Delegation choice:** The right to select and change representatives.
- **Governance proposals:** The right to participate in the submission and consideration of governance actions, subject to applicable requirements.
- **Custodial arrangements:** The ability to authorize, or withhold authorization from, third-party custodians to exercise governance rights on their behalf.

ADA ownership does not automatically require active governance participation. Holders may choose whether and how to exercise their governance rights, subject to the applicable protocol rules.

These rights are not exclusive to individuals who perform other governance roles. An ADA holder may also operate a stake pool, register as a DRep, or participate in other ecosystem activities.

### Voting Power and Stake Delegation

Cardano uses a stake-weighted governance model in which voting power is proportional to the ADA associated with eligible stake credentials.

Voting power is therefore not distributed equally among wallet addresses or individual participants. Its weight depends on the amount of ADA represented, according to the protocol's stake distribution and snapshot rules.

Two independent delegation mechanisms operate within Cardano:

- **Stake delegation:** Assigns stake to a stake pool, contributing to block production and the pool's stake-based governance voting power.
- **Governance delegation:** Assigns governance voting power to a DRep or one of the predefined delegation options.

These mechanisms serve different purposes and can be used simultaneously without requiring the same recipient.

For example, an ADA holder may delegate stake to one pool for block production while delegating governance voting power to an unrelated DRep.

Neither operation transfers ownership of ADA. Assets remain under the holder's control unless separate custodial arrangements apply.

## 2. Governance Delegation and Participation

### How Governance Delegation Works

Governance delegation allows ADA holders to determine how their voting power is represented in Cardano's on-chain decision-making process.

Delegation is performed through a transaction that associates the holder's stake credential with a registered DRep credential or a predefined governance delegation option.

When voting power is delegated to a DRep, that representative can cast votes on eligible governance actions using the voting power associated with the delegating stake.

The DRep does not acquire ownership or custody of the delegated ADA. Governance delegation also does not prevent holders from using, transferring, or otherwise managing their assets.

A DRep's voting power can change over time as delegators transfer ADA, change their governance delegation, or otherwise modify the stake associated with their credentials.

The protocol uses stake distribution snapshots when calculating voting power for governance decisions.

### Choosing a DRep

ADA holders can select representatives according to their own priorities, governance preferences, and expectations.

Registered DReps may publish information about their objectives, motivations, qualifications, and positions on governance matters. Their historical voting records and published vote rationales can provide additional information about their participation.

Depending on the available platforms, delegators may examine indicators such as:

- **Voting participation:** The proportion of eligible governance actions on which a DRep has voted.
- **Voting history:** Previous Yes, No, and Abstain votes on specific governance actions.
- **Vote rationales:** Explanations published in connection with voting decisions.
- **Voting power:** The amount of ADA represented by the DRep.
- **Delegator count:** The number of stake credentials delegating voting power to the representative, as reported by the platform.
- **Activity status:** Whether the DRep is active, inactive, or retired.
- **Governance positions:** The alignment between a representative's historical decisions and the delegator's preferences.
- **Profile information:** Publicly stated objectives, motivations, qualifications, and external references.

These indicators provide different perspectives on a representative's activity and should not be interpreted as interchangeable measures of performance.

For example, high voting participation indicates activity but does not establish the quality of individual decisions. Similarly, publishing a rationale does not necessarily mean that its arguments are complete or well supported.

Governance platforms may also calculate similar indicators differently, depending on their methodologies, data coverage, and update frequency.

### DRep Explorers and Directories

Several public platforms allow ADA holders to discover representatives, examine their profiles, and review governance activity.

**DRepTalk**

[DRep Directory](https://dreptalk.com/dreps/) | [Find Your DRep](https://dreptalk.com/match/)

Provides a DRep directory with delegated voting power, delegator counts, voting records, rationales, and governance activity.

Its *Find Your DRep* feature allows users to compare their preferences on selected historical governance actions with representatives' actual voting records.

**CardanoCube**

[DRep Explorer](https://www.cardanocube.com/governance/dreps)

Provides a searchable directory with filters for activity status, profile information, voting power, delegator counts, and rationale coverage.

Additional filtering capabilities allow users to explore representatives across different levels of delegated voting power.

**CGOV - Cardano Governance Platform**

[DRep Dashboard](https://app.cgov.io/drep) | [DRep Charts](https://app.cgov.io/drep/charts)

Provides information about DRep activity, delegated ADA, delegator counts, voting statistics, and voting power distribution.

Its visualizations help users examine individual representatives and broader patterns of governance participation.

**Cardano GovTool**

[Governance Platform](https://gov.tools/) | [Delegation Guide](https://docs.gov.tools/cardano-govtool/using-govtool/delegating/delegate-to-a-drep/)

Provides a directory where users can search for DReps by name or identifier and examine available profiles, voting power, and registration information.

The platform also allows users to delegate voting power through a compatible wallet.

**DAVEgov - Cardano Governance Visualisation**

[Governance Explorer](https://governance.cardano-visualisation.com/)

Provides visualizations of DRep voting behavior, participation rates, voting positions, rationale coverage, and activity across different governance action types.

**Cardanoscan**

[DRep Explorer](https://cardanoscan.io/dreps) | [Governance Overview](https://cardanoscan.io/gov/overview)

Provides on-chain information about registered DReps, including identifiers, registration records, activity status, and governance transactions.

Its governance overview also presents aggregate information about participation and voting power.

The platforms listed above are examples of available resources rather than an exhaustive directory.

### Delegating and Changing Representatives

ADA holders can delegate their governance voting power through compatible wallets, governance platforms, or technical tools capable of constructing governance delegation transactions.

A typical delegation process involves:

1. **Selecting a representative:** Identify the DRep and confirm their DRep ID.
2. **Choosing a delegation method:** Use a compatible wallet or governance platform.
3. **Preparing the transaction:** Create a governance delegation transaction associating the stake credential with the chosen DRep.
4. **Authorizing the transaction:** Review and sign it using the appropriate wallet credentials.
5. **Submitting and confirming:** Submit the transaction and verify that the delegation was recorded on-chain.

Some wallets provide governance delegation directly through their interfaces. Others require users to connect their wallets to external governance platforms.

For example, the [GovTool delegation guide](https://docs.gov.tools/cardano-govtool/using-govtool/delegating/delegate-to-a-drep/) documents a process in which users search for a DRep, connect a wallet, authorize the transaction, and confirm the delegation.

A registered stake credential can change its governance delegation by submitting a new delegation transaction.

The new delegation replaces the previous association. There is no need to withdraw ADA or change the existing stake pool delegation.

A transaction fee applies, and additional registration deposits may be required when a stake credential has not yet been registered.

Governance delegation never requires disclosing a wallet recovery phrase to a website or representative.

### Abstain and No Confidence

In addition to registered DReps, Cardano provides two predefined governance delegation options.

**Abstain**

The *Abstain* option allows an ADA holder to register a standing preference not to participate in governance voting.

Stake delegated to this option is excluded from the active voting stake used in the applicable governance action approval calculations.

This provides a way to establish a governance delegation without assigning voting power to a specific representative.

**No Confidence**

The *No Confidence* option expresses a standing position of no confidence in the Constitutional Committee.

Under the rules defined by CIP-1694, stake delegated to this option is treated as:

- A **Yes** vote on *Motion of No Confidence* governance actions.
- A **No** vote on other governance actions.

Unlike *Abstain*, this stake remains part of the active governance voting stake.

These predefined options are handled by the protocol rather than by representatives manually submitting votes.

They should also be distinguished from an individual *Abstain* vote cast by a registered DRep on a particular governance action.

ADA holders can change between these predefined options and registered DReps through governance delegation transactions.

### Direct Voting

ADA holders who wish to cast governance votes themselves may register as DReps and delegate their voting power to their own governance credentials.

This allows them to vote directly on governance actions without relying on another representative's decisions.

Some governance platforms, including GovTool, refer to this arrangement as *Direct Voting*.

However, *Direct Voter* is a platform-level designation rather than a separate role defined by the Cardano protocol. On-chain, direct voters use the same registration and voting mechanisms as DReps.

DRep registration requires a refundable deposit defined by the `drepDeposit` protocol parameter, in addition to applicable transaction fees.

As of October 2026, this deposit is 500 ADA. Its value is governance-controlled and may change.

For additional information about registration, DRep credentials, voting procedures, and retirement, see [DReps - Delegated Representatives](../dreps/).

### Monitoring Governance Delegation

Selecting a DRep does not prevent ADA holders from reviewing their representative's subsequent activity.

Governance explorers and directories provide information that can help delegators monitor voting records, published rationales, participation, registration status, and changes in delegated voting power.

Some platforms also offer notifications or tracking features related to a representative's governance activity.

Monitoring a DRep over time can provide additional context beyond the information available when the initial delegation was made.

ADA holders remain free to reassess their delegation preferences and change representatives whenever they choose.

### Governance Delegation and Staking Rewards

Under the governance rules in effect as of October 2026, withdrawing staking rewards requires the associated stake credential to have a governance delegation.

This requirement can be satisfied by delegating to:

- A registered DRep.
- The predefined *Abstain* option.
- The predefined *No Confidence* option.

The requirement applies to withdrawing staking rewards, not to holding or transferring ADA.

Rewards can continue to accumulate even when a qualifying governance delegation has not been established. However, withdrawing them requires satisfying the applicable delegation condition.

[CIP-181 - Remove DRep Requirement for Reward Withdrawals](https://cips.cardano.org/cip/CIP-0181), proposed in March 2026, seeks to remove this condition.

As of October 2026, the proposal has not been implemented, and the existing requirement remains in effect.
